Changelog

Highlights

Oxygon started in July 2026 as a pace calculator and became a place to plan a race, record it, and share it.

  • Plan the course, the pace and the fuelling, checkpoint by checkpoint.
  • Record with GPS, heart rate and auto-pause, and send your crew a live link.
  • Bring training in from Garmin, through intervals.icu, and from Strava. Share a finished recording back to Strava.
  • Run, ride, swim, lift, practice yoga, or sit in the sauna. Each one is its own session.
  • Follow people you train with. A post can carry photos, a chart, a sticker, a story, or a 3D replay.
  • The calendar holds the month, the week, and a note on a day. On iPhone, widgets and the Lock Screen follow the session.
  • A Season plan, and an export of your activities, are on the web from 30 October. They reach Android, iPhone and iPad with the next app versions. Android is 1 November 2026.

1.3.11 — 30 October 2026

On Google Play. Not an App Store version. The App Store app is still 1.3.10. The web app, from 30 October, has the automatic Strava upload, Season plan and export.

New

  • On Google Play and on the web, a finished recording uploads to Strava on its own, and the upload retries until it succeeds. On the phone this still happens if you close the app right after Finish. The switch starts off. An activity you imported still waits for Share. An outdoor share includes the route. The App Store version on sale, 1.3.10, does not upload on its own. There you share from the activity.
  • Add photos and video to any finished activity, Sauna included.
  • That week shows Run pace, with Climb under it. Climb is the runs' total ascent in metres, beside the week before.
  • Season plan, on the web from 30 October. Android, iPhone and iPad receive it with the next app versions. Android is 1 November 2026. It is not in App Store 1.3.10. One main goal race, and other races if you want them. Each week has a load target, with hours beside it for context. The rhythm can be three loading weeks and one recovery week. A missed or heavy week is explained, and you choose whether to keep the targets or review the rest of the season. Nothing moves on its own. The first week starts from the lower of two readings of your recent training, and you may lower that number.
  • Export your activities, on the web from 30 October. Android, iPhone and iPad receive it with the next app versions. Android is 1 November 2026. It is not in App Store 1.3.10. You start from the calendar, the list or the charts, and you choose the dates. The file is built on your device, or in the browser on the web, and it is not assembled on a server. It can include each activity, a table of the activities, and a training report as a PDF. Photos and video stay out unless you turn them on.

Improved

  • Heart rate keeps recording when a strap drops out.
  • The month lists each sport you have done, and the time still planned sits beside it in grey.
  • Sauna, yoga and strength are their own sessions. A sauna fills toward the time you set. During a sauna you can change the temperature, and a finished sauna or yoga shows its heart-rate chart.
  • Charts open more smoothly. A button expands a chart. On the full chart, the pace lane leaves a break where you stopped, instead of stretching across the stop. The That week numbers line up in one table.
  • On the web, someone who opens your live link after you have started sees the route so far, not only where you are now.

Fixed

  • A run with no distance says Not recorded.
  • A live run stays open.
  • On the web, a day note no longer collides with the date on the calendar.

Platforms

  • Google Play has the automatic Strava upload and retry, photos and video, Run pace and Climb, the heart-rate hold, the grey planned time, sauna, yoga and strength, the chart button and the pace-lane break, Not recorded, and a live run that stays open. The web has the same Strava upload. App Store 1.3.10 does not.
  • Season plan and export are on the web from 30 October. Android, iPhone and iPad receive them with the next app versions. Android is 1 November 2026. They are not in App Store 1.3.10.
  • The route so far on a live link is on the web. It is not in the App Store version on sale today.
  • Widgets, the Lock Screen and the Dynamic Island stay on iPhone.

Web — 24 to 29 October 2026

On the web app at my.oxygon.app, in the days before the 30 October update. The App Store version 1.3.10, on sale from 28 October, includes the items marked for iPhone.

New

  • That week shows your average run pace, or your ride speed when the week has no runs, and how it compares with the week before. The current week includes the sessions you have planned. A past week compares what you did. A future week compares what you have planned.
  • Race Predictor sets every distance from 5K to the marathon, plus 60K, 80K and 100K, from your best recent run. One line names that run. The same predictor is in the App Store version.
  • Share a story, a sticker, or a 3D video. Stickers can be your stats, the route, the splits, the day, a big number, or a map. A story can show how this workout compares with last time, or how a run moved your race prediction. A ride can share an effort fingerprint, ridge lines, or a short sound piece.
  • A wellness day can show weight, resting heart rate, sleep and heart-rate variability. Open the day for the charts. A day with nothing recorded shows no block.
  • A step can target a heart rate, on a run, a ride or a swim. Planned load learns from recent steady sessions of the same kind.
  • You can set the fuelling temperature by hand, or fetch the forecast for that start.
  • The pace calculator keeps favourites and a split chart. Critical speed can take a third trial.

Improved

  • Cardiac drift draws a straight trend line, with a dot for each repetition, and names the warm-up and the cool-down. A finished run can share that drift as a picture. On a live session the heart-rate chart draws the same line once the warm-up is over.
  • Save or close returns you to the day you were editing, not to today. The week column has the only Today control.
  • Done versus planned lists each sport by the hours you planned, most first.
  • A Community post can open the activity, including a sauna, yoga or strength session, so you can edit it.
  • Tile titles stay on one line, so the values line up. Charts, the 3D video and the share summary follow kilometres or miles, whichever you use.
  • Connected apps names Strava once the account is connected, and says where sharing starts before you turn it on. Share on a finished run is a switch for Community and a switch for Strava. Community comes down if you turn it off. Strava stays up once it has been posted, and removing it is done on Strava.
  • A long ride's replay clip is ready to share when playback ends. Swipe between stories. The share sheet keeps the other cards if one of them fails.
  • Bikes can be tagged road, gravel, mountain, indoor or race. A ride with no bike takes yours. A bike you chose by hand stays.
  • Search in Settings is no longer cut off on a phone. The Charts page draws each chart once.

Fixed

  • A run with no distance says Not recorded.
  • A missing figure is left blank. Empty lap columns are hidden.
  • An empty course no longer shows a stand-in for its time.
  • In a 3D video, a missing heart-rate reading stays a gap.
  • A removed post, or one that is no longer shared with you, says so in plain words.
  • Typing a time in Race Predictor keeps every digit you enter.
  • Choosing a heart-rate target again keeps the band you already set.
  • Incline waits until there is enough distance to draw it. Letting go after a hold keeps the chart menu open.

Platforms

  • This section is the web app.
  • iPhone and iPad received the matching pieces that were in the App Store version of 28 October: the predictor, the stories and stickers, the share switches, the sauna temperature and heart-rate chart, and returning to the day you edited. See 1.3.10. The drift line on the heart-rate chart is on the web in this stretch.
  • Anything dated here after that App Store version is on the web first.

1.3.10 — 28 October 2026

On the App Store. This is the update after 1.3.3. The web app already had most of it through October. Google Play did not receive this version as its own release. Play moved from 1.3.6 to 1.3.11 on 30 October.

New

  • Plan a run, ride or swim on one page. Add Sections, a Course, Pacing or Fuel as you need them, and mark it as a race with one switch. Open it from the calendar ready to start. A Fuel block puts the doses on the plan and on the live screen.
  • The calendar can hold a note for a day or several days: limited availability, no availability, illness, injury, recovery, or a plain note. A color band marks those days. A missed plan keeps its color and gains a ring once the day is over.
  • When you finish, post to Community or share to Strava. You choose what goes out, including hiding the ends of the route. Read and accept the Community rules before the first post. You can report or block a post.
  • Share a 3D video of a run or a ride. The camera follows the route through the turns. Or share a sticker.
  • Race Predictor uses one recent run for every distance it offers, and names that run.
  • On iPhone, Home Screen widgets follow the session and your plans. The Lock Screen and the Dynamic Island show distance, pace, time and heart rate during a session.
  • Send a bug, an idea or a question from any screen, including the live screen. The note shows which screen you were on.
  • After an update, What's New opens once. You can open it again from Settings, under Help.

Improved

  • Every planned activity uses the same step layout. Save and close is on every activity type. Going back with unsaved changes asks you to discard them.
  • Month tiles show a circle for each activity, in its color, with the time or the distance inside. A separate recording can take the plan's place. Weeks run continuously across the month.
  • The same sport, started within a few minutes, becomes one activity. When a watch and the phone both recorded it, the Garmin figures are the ones that count. Notes, photos and fuelling stay. You can set a recording aside or bring it back.
  • A step tile counts down to the next target and shows a finish flag on the last step. It flashes through the last few seconds. A heart-rate workout shows the next zone. The planned pace sits beside Now, in grey.
  • Tap Pace for the current and the average pace, and for speed. Tap Distance for what is left. Tap Energy for the current rate or the average, and for active or gross calories. With heart rate, energy can split into carbohydrate and fat. Hold a tile to move it, including before you start.
  • On iPad, the readings sit on top and both charts sit full width below. Hold a chart to read a moment.
  • A ride replay lasts at least as long as you were moving, and the bike eases through the turns. Kilometre markers on a ride replay do not overlap.
  • Connect a heart-rate strap, or a watch that broadcasts heart rate, plus a treadmill or a foot pod, a Wahoo KICKR, and power and cadence pedals. A saved heart-rate source connects on its own, including when it starts after you do. On an indoor run, distance and pace can come from the treadmill or the foot pod. On an indoor ride, speed can come from power.
  • The sauna ring fills toward the time you set. You can change the temperature during the session. A finished sauna or yoga shows its heart-rate chart.
  • Strength sessions use the exercise library, including flywheel exercises. Each set row has its own reps and weight. A flywheel set uses a wheel size. Search by equipment or muscle group.
  • A finished activity opens from the record saved with it. You can correct a treadmill or foot-pod distance, how it felt, and the note. A Garmin distance stays. Share sits with the rest of the page, after Replay.
  • Oxygon asks you to name an activity you recorded in the app. An imported activity keeps the name it arrived with.
  • Each tile is spoken as one item.
  • Photos you add stay in Oxygon. A share to Strava does not take them from the app.

Fixed

  • The finish review stays where it is when you turn the phone, and the finished page opens after you save or leave it.
  • Signing in tries once more if the first attempt fails. A note you already saved is not sent again.
  • The calendar stays on the week you were looking at when you save, move or delete.
  • Finish works on the first tap. Closing an activity returns you to that day.
  • An outdoor run shows the ascent you climbed. A ride can show its drift and its heart-rate zones.
  • The trainer's plus and minus step once per tap.

Platforms

  • iPhone and iPad, on the App Store. Widgets, the Lock Screen and the Dynamic Island are iPhone only.
  • The web app had the plans, the calendar, Community, the replay and the sensors' web mirror through October. See the web section above for what the browser gained on its own in the last week of the month.
  • Google Play's public app on 28 October was still 1.3.6. What Play gained on 30 October is in 1.3.11.

July 2026

Where Oxygon started. It began as a pace calculator for iPhone on 11 July 2026. What this month built is what people installed in August.

New

  • Pace Calculator. Enter pace, time or distance, any two, and the third is filled in. Splits open as a full-screen sheet, and the whole row is tappable. Presets cover the usual race distances, on through 100K. A favourite keeps a pace you use often, with a name and a tag. Favourites sync once you have an account.
  • The rest of Tools landed in the same two weeks. All of them work with no account and no connection. Grade-Adjusted Pace. Heart Rate Zones, with the method those zones use. Training Paces. Calories Burned. Race Predictor. VO2max Estimator, which estimates from a recent result. Critical Speed, from the trials you enter.
  • Settings hold the units, the accent color and a profile.
  • iPad has its own layouts, including a sidebar with the same destinations as the phone's tabs. A window can use the large iPhone size.
  • The planner. A course comes from a GPX file, kept on the device, or from a sample course when you have no file. Distance quick-picks sit beside it. The course and the plan stay in step, and a prompt asks when the file's distance and the plan's distance disagree. An elevation profile shows the climb, the checkpoints and a marker for where you are. Checkpoints begin about every 5 km. You can rename one, pin one so it stays put, or lock a hand edit. Each leg shows its own ascent and descent, and the course shows the total.
  • Fuelling on that plan. The planner holds more than one race. Drinks and food sit on the checkpoints, including when the caffeine lands. Scan a barcode to add a product, and the products you keep stay in a library on the device.
  • The live page, for the session itself. This is the in-app page, separate from the Lock Screen Live Activity that came in September. GPS keeps your position while the phone is locked, with the next checkpoint and whether you are ahead of the plan or behind it. The chart is the course with heart rate on it, and the same chart is there while you are still planning. Connect a heart-rate strap, or a watch that broadcasts heart rate, and the page shows the rate and the trend. Weather for the course can sit on the chart. The crew page reads in whole kilometres, with labelled ranges, so someone with the link can see where you are.
  • The race library. Real races, with a map and filters for distance, terrain, sport and country, plus sorting and a way back to the top. Pick a race and the planner takes it. A course distance that cannot be right is refused, so a wrong file does not trap the plan.
  • Strava, as an import. Workouts come in, and the route comes with them. Apple Health can bring workouts in as well.
  • An account is optional. It is how plans sync between devices. You can delete the account from inside the app.

Improved

  • The planner's steps collapse into one course-and-method step, so a race is one pass.
  • The Events tab uses a crossed-flags mark. On iPad the sidebar carries those same destinations.
  • The last kilometre can be the headline pace, with the average for the run beside it.

Fixed

  • Opening a course in the planner no longer closes the app.
  • Grade-Adjusted Pace accepts an elevation above 1,000 m.

Platforms

  • iPhone and iPad. No public Android app yet, and no public web app yet. Android reached people on 28 September. The web app opened through late August and September.
